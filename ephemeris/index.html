01 What are we actually looking at?

The barycenter is the shared center of mass of the solar system. The planets pull on the Sun, too. Their changing positions move this point relative to the Sun's center, at times beyond its surface. The orbital view is a two-dimensional projection; boundary status uses all three coordinates.

02 Where does the signal come from?

Ephemerides are requested from NASA JPL's Horizons system ↗, using the solar system barycenter as the target and the Sun's center as the origin. These are computed ephemerides, rather than a live telescope feed. The status strip identifies the source of the displayed data.

03 What happens when contact is lost?

If the request fails, the terminal displays a simplified simulation, explicitly marked as demonstration data. It is not suitable for astronomical conclusions. The latest plotted epoch may precede the end of your window, depending on the chosen sampling interval.
