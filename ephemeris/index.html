01. What is this notebook for?

To augment conventional Western astrology with speculative transits and cycles drawn from both the solar-system barycenter and the Milky Way's barycenter, alongside related measurements at those scales. The local instrument is the starting point. A Galactic mass-center model and its relation to a Western chart remain part of the research. Computed positions and proposed astrological interpretations are recorded separately.

02. What does this version actually measure?

The current instrument plots the solar system's center of mass relative to the center of the Sun. The orbital view shows its X–Y projection; the boundary test uses all three coordinates. NASA's vectors use the ecliptic of J2000.0, with epochs in TDB. These are solar-system relative vectors, not positions measured from the galactic center. Acceleration is derived from successive velocity samples.

03. Sources / reference frames

The computed ephemerides come from NASA JPL Horizons. See its API parameters for vector origins and reference planes. For the galactic layer, Astropy's Galactocentric frame documentation describes the parameters needed to choose a galactic origin and solar motion. A coordinate transformation alone does not compute the Galaxy's center of mass. The McMillan Milky Way mass model is a research lead for the dynamical layer. No Galactic barycenter or orbit is calculated in this version.

04. If the source goes quiet

A failed request produces a simplified simulation, labeled as demonstration data. It is not an astronomical ephemeris. The latest plotted epoch can precede the end of the requested window, depending on the sampling interval.
