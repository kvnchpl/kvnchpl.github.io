01. What is this notebook for?

To extend Western astrology through the solar-system barycenter, the Milky Way's barycenter, and the cycles that connect them. Astrology is the ground of this work. The inquiry develops its measures, its language, and the relationships between human and galactic time.

02. The body within the field

This astrology begins with the effects of moving celestial masses on the body, through their shifting gravitational relationships. The barycenter traces their changing configuration. The one-year field instrument follows external acceleration, tidal stretching and compression, and the axes through which their relationships turn. Spacetime and quantum relationships open further lines of inquiry within this material account.

03. What does this version actually measure?

The solar instrument plots the system's center of mass relative to the Sun. Its orbital view shows the X–Y projection; its boundary test uses all three coordinates, and acceleration is derived from successive velocity samples. The separate year record computes the external field and tidal tensor at Earth center, using Sun, Moon, and planetary-system positions and gravitational parameters. Both records use geometric ecliptic J2000 / ICRF vectors at TDB epochs. The Galactic wheel supplies a reference-date phase snapshot alongside them.

04. Sources / reference frames

The computed ephemerides come from NASA JPL Horizons. See its API parameters for vector origins and reference planes. For the galactic layer, Astropy's Galactocentric frame documentation describes the parameters needed to choose a galactic origin and solar motion. A coordinate transformation alone does not compute the Galaxy's center of mass. The McMillan Milky Way mass model is a research lead for the dynamical layer. The phase wheel uses an adopted dynamical center and a Sun proxy with constant angular rates; a full Galactic mass-center and orbital model remains to develop.

05. An inherited sky

Nut's encompassing sky, the passage and renewal of the Sun, and the zodiac meet in the cultural research behind this astrology. The coffin of Soter, around 100–120 CE, brings Nut and zodiac imagery into one field. Ephemeris develops this language of enclosure, passage, and renewal within a new galactic wheel.

06. If the source goes quiet

A failed solar query produces a simplified simulation, labeled as demonstration data. It is not an astronomical ephemeris. The latest plotted epoch can precede the end of the requested window, depending on the sampling interval. The fixed field-year record is separate: if it cannot load, retry to receive it.

07. Renew the instruments

Astrology inherits a long practice of observing, calculating, and reading the sky. Medieval astronomy and astrology shared instruments and intersected with medicine and natural philosophy, as traced in Oxford's history of the study of the stars. The work continues. Newly measurable celestial relationships enter the chart; the inherited language grows with them.
