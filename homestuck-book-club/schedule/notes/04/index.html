Reading overview

Hivebent starts over with twelve trolls on Alternia. The former chat-window antagonists become the main cast, with their own interests, lusii, feuds, romances, and competing plans for Sgrub. Their supposed two-team competition ultimately forms one connected session.

The group wins its battle against the Black King and creates the humans’ universe, but an intruding demon prevents them from claiming their reward. They retreat to the Veil. Their anger at the humans now has a context, though blame and causality remain much messier than Karkat’s insults suggested.

Reading notes

  • TROLL INTRODUCTIONS YAY. I have been waiting for the drama to take over. Karkat’s passion for terrible romantic movies makes him feel much closer to John than his earlier trolling suggested. Watching his attempts at leadership adds another layer of awkwardness. The change of perspective humanizes him, for lack of a better word.
  • Relationships get more than one category. The quadrant explanation groups romantic, friendly, and hostile relationships into one scheme; it even makes mediation a formal role. I like the alternative to treating romantic love as the only relationship that deserves a name. That does not mean the categories necessarily protect anyone from coercion or hurt.
  • Equius’s control is gross. His policing of Nepeta’s language and his attempt to program Aradia’s feelings are worth discussing as specific acts of control. Nepeta also sees through some of his performance; she is not simply oblivious to it.
  • The hierarchy is not just decoration. Equius’s deference and prejudices, Eridan’s ambitions, and Feferi’s stated hopes do not amount to the same attitude toward Alternia. The system constrains the others even when they resist it; caring about hierarchy is not limited to those it benefits most.
  • Knowing the future does not make you innocent. Doc Scratch presents his manipulations as barely participating in what was inevitable. Aradia’s memos make future selves another source of instructions. Who gets to describe a choice as already settled?
  • Moirail breakup :( Eridan offers to drop his typing accent to show he is serious, and Feferi answers in plain letters too. I love how stepping out of the typing bit becomes an emotional gesture. If we made a club shipping wall, I would want it to show one-sided feelings, contested labels, and changing relationships, not just declare every pairing mutual.

Discussion questions

  1. Which troll became most interesting to you over the course of the act?
  2. Which relationship between the trolls feels most central to the group?
  3. Which feature of Alternian society most shaped your understanding of the trolls?
  4. Which character changed the most from your initial impression of them?
  5. How does Karkat's idea of leadership compare with the way the group actually functions?
  6. How does experiencing the trolls from their own perspective change the meaning of their earlier interactions with the humans?
  7. How does Alternian hierarchy influence the trolls' relationships with one another?
  8. How does Aradia's movement between different forms affect the way her identity is presented?
  9. How does the quadrant system shape the way the trolls understand their emotions?
  10. How does the group's history complicate the distinction between friendship and hostility?
  11. What kinds of social hierarchy does Alternia seem designed to examine? Where do the trolls reproduce or resist those hierarchies?
  12. What does the quadrant system suggest about the effect of naming and categorizing relationships?
  13. How does Trollian change the relationship between conversation and chronological time?
  14. What does the trolls' interrupted victory suggest about the promise that games reward successful play?
  15. How does learning that the trolls created the humans' universe change the relationship between the two storylines?
  16. Where does a quadrant label help a character understand their feelings, and where does it become an excuse to demand something from another person?
  17. How does Feferi’s exhaustion complicate the idea that caring for someone means being responsible for managing them?

Trivia & Details

  • The reset begins with familiar ages. The opening translates six solar sweeps into thirteen Earth years. The parallel is explicit, even before the characters’ introductions start echoing the kids’ rooms and interests.
  • The names have a visible pattern. Each of the twelve introduced trolls has a six-letter first name and a six-letter surname. Their trolltag initials also stay within A, C, G, and T, the letters used for DNA’s four bases. Try checking the pattern against the introductions.
  • Karkat’s outdoor monologue rewrites John’s. Compare John’s lyrical monologue with Karkat dismissing poetry and mailbox arms. The echo is in the wording, not just the visual setup.
  • Kanaya’s assumptions are not a chronology lesson. She imagines Rose’s walkthrough comes from an ancient alien civilization. That is her inference from finding the journal, not a reliable date for Rose’s session.
