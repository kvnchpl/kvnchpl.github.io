Reading overview

Life on Alternia

  • Hivebent introduces Karkat and eleven other trolls on Alternia. They grow up with animal guardians called lusii.
  • Sollux adapts technology recovered by Aradia into Sgrub. The players organize rival Red and Blue Teams.

Feuds and Romance

  • The four quadrants distinguish attraction, rivalry, protective partnerships, and mediation in troll romance.
  • Vriska’s attacks leave Tavros paralyzed and Aradia dead. Doc Scratch encourages Vriska’s revenge against her friends.
  • Aradia merges with her sprite and enters a robotic body built by Equius. She rejects his attempt to program her feelings for him.
  • Feferi ends her moirallegiance with Eridan, exhausted by looking after him and keeping his destructive plans in check.

Playing Sgrub

  • The Red and Blue Teams turn out to be two entry chains in one session. All twelve players reach the Medium.
  • Most of the trolls prototype their sprites with their dead lusii, giving the enemy royalty the same creatures’ powers.
  • Aradia gathers an army of doomed alternate selves for the final battle with the Black King.

Victory Interrupted

  • The group narrowly defeats the Black King and gathers at a door leading to the game’s reward.
  • A demon enters through a rift before they can claim the reward. The Aradiabot army is destroyed while the others escape to the Veil.
  • From a laboratory in the Veil, the trolls contact the humans they blame for the disaster. Their game created the universe containing Earth and the four kids.

Reading notes

  • TROLL INTRODUCTIONS YAY. I have been waiting for the drama to take over. Karkat’s passion for terrible romantic movies makes him feel much closer to John than his earlier trolling suggested. Watching his attempts at leadership adds another layer of awkwardness. The change of perspective humanizes him, for lack of a better word.
  • Relationships get more than one category. The quadrant explanation treats attraction, hostility, and forms of care that humans might call platonic as parts of troll romance. It even gives mediation its own relationship role. I like seeing so much importance given to relationships beyond a conventional romantic couple. Having a name for a relationship does not, by itself, make it caring or mutual.
  • Equius’s control is gross. His policing of Nepeta’s language and his attempt to program Aradia’s feelings are worth discussing as specific acts of control. Nepeta also challenges his claim to be above her games: she points out how much he plays with his own typing style. She notices his hypocrisy even when he insists on having the last word.
  • The hierarchy is not just decoration. Alternia’s social ranks shape how the trolls treat one another. Equius combines prejudice with deference to those above him, while Eridan and Feferi want very different futures for their society. Their arguments reveal both support for Alternia’s hierarchy and resistance to it.
  • Who gets to call something inevitable? Doc Scratch downplays his manipulations as small nudges toward events that would happen anyway. Yet he is actively encouraging Vriska to hurt people. Aradia’s memos raise a different problem: even her own future selves become authorities telling her what to do. How much room does either version of inevitability leave for a choice?
  • Moirail breakup :( Eridan offers to drop his typing accent to show he is serious, and Feferi answers in plain letters too. I love how stepping out of the typing bit becomes an emotional gesture. A shipping wall could track one-sided feelings, contested labels, and changing relationships without treating every pairing as mutual.

Discussion questions

  1. Which troll did you change your mind about most once we saw their life on Alternia?
  2. Which relationship did you find yourself most invested in, and why?
  3. How much credit does Karkat deserve for holding the group together?
  4. Who or what do you think the demon is, and how might it be connected to the humans’ session?
  5. What stays recognizably Aradia as her form changes, and what seems to change with it?
  6. When do quadrant labels clarify what the trolls want from each other, and when do they obscure it?
  7. What does Feferi owe Eridan as his moirail, and where should that responsibility end?
  8. How does Alternia’s hierarchy show up in ordinary conversations? Where do the trolls defend it, resist it, or take it for granted?
  9. What did the trolls have to do to win Sgrub, and why were they unable to claim the reward? What does that outcome suggest about the game’s promise of success?

Trivia & Details

  • Hivebent begins with familiar ages. The opening translates six solar sweeps into thirteen Earth years. The trolls are the same age as the kids, even before their introductions start echoing the kids’ rooms and interests.
  • The names have a visible pattern. Each of the twelve introduced trolls has a six-letter first name and a six-letter surname. Their trolltag initials also stay within A, C, G, and T, the letters used for DNA’s four bases.
  • Karkat’s outdoor monologue rewrites John’s. Compare John’s lyrical monologue with Karkat dismissing poetry and mailbox arms.
  • Kanaya admires Rose’s walkthrough. She imagines it describes an ancient alien civilization. Its author is another young player, still learning how the game works.
