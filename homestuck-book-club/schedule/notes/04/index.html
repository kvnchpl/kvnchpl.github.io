Reading overview

Life on Alternia

  • Hivebent introduces Karkat and eleven other trolls on Alternia. They grow up with animal guardians called lusii.
  • Sollux adapts technology recovered by Aradia into Sgrub. The players organize rival Red and Blue Teams.

Feuds and Romance

  • The four quadrants distinguish attraction, rivalry, protective partnerships, and mediation in troll romance.
  • Vriska’s attacks leave Tavros paralyzed and Aradia dead. Doc Scratch encourages Vriska’s revenge against her friends.
  • Aradia merges with her sprite and enters a robotic body built by Equius. She rejects his attempt to program her feelings for him.
  • Feferi ends her moirallegiance with Eridan, exhausted by looking after him and keeping his destructive plans in check.

Playing Sgrub

  • The Red and Blue Teams turn out to be two entry chains in one session. All twelve players reach the Medium.
  • Most of the trolls prototype their sprites with their dead lusii, giving the enemy royalty the same creatures’ powers.
  • Aradia gathers an army of doomed alternate selves for the final battle with the Black King.

Victory Interrupted

  • The group narrowly defeats the Black King and gathers at a door leading to the game’s reward.
  • A demon enters through a rift before they can claim the reward. The Aradiabot army is destroyed while the others escape to the Veil.
  • From a laboratory in the Veil, the trolls contact the humans they blame for the disaster. Their game created the universe containing Earth and the four kids.

Reading notes

  • TROLL INTRODUCTIONS YAY. The troll introductions bring the relationships behind the earlier trolling into focus. Karkat’s passion for terrible romantic movies makes him feel much closer to John than his insults suggested. Watching his attempts at leadership adds another layer of awkwardness. The change of perspective humanizes him, for lack of a better word.
  • Relationships get more than one category. The quadrant explanation treats attraction, hostility, and forms of care that humans might call platonic as parts of troll romance. It even gives mediation its own relationship role. I like seeing so much importance given to relationships beyond a conventional romantic couple. Having a name for a relationship does not, by itself, make it caring or mutual.
  • Equius’s control is gross. He blames Nepeta’s swearing on her lower-blooded friends and insists she needs him to look after her. Nepeta sees through his disdain for her roleplay: he puts plenty of effort into his own typing games. His attempt to program Aradia’s feelings takes that control further. He treats his judgment as more important than what either of them wants.
  • Who gets to call something inevitable? Doc Scratch downplays his manipulations as small nudges toward events that would happen anyway. Yet he is actively encouraging Vriska to hurt people. Aradia’s memos raise a different problem: even her own future selves become authorities telling her what to do. How much room does either version of inevitability leave for a choice?
  • Moirail breakup :( Eridan offers to drop his typing accent to show he is serious, and Feferi answers in plain letters too. I love how stepping out of the typing bit becomes an emotional gesture. It still doesn’t give them the same understanding of the relationship: he appeals to their destiny as moirails, while she says both people have to want it.

Open threads

These threads are unresolved at the end of this assignment.

  • The Scratch. Aradia traces the rift to four players in another session. The story hasn’t shown what leads those players to open it or what they hope it will do.
  • The reward out of reach. The trolls’ game has created a new universe, but the demon prevents them from entering it. How they can escape the Veil and whether they have any way to reach that universe remain unresolved.
  • The trolls and the humans. The trolls’ session created the universe the kids live in. The trolls blame the humans for what happened to them; how much the two groups can help each other is still open.

Discussion questions

  1. Which troll surprised you most once you saw what their life on Alternia was like?
  2. Why do the Red and Blue Teams seem to be playing separate games at first? How do their entry chains come together?
  3. Aradia says four players in another session open the rift. Who or what do you think comes through it? What clues are you going on?
  4. What changes when Aradia moves from ghost to sprite to robot? Which changes seem to come from the body, and which might come from what she has been through?
  5. Which of the troll quadrants feels closest to a relationship people recognize in real life? Where does the comparison get harder?
  6. Feferi ends her moirallegiance with Eridan and offers to stay friends. Where do you draw the line between caring about someone and taking responsibility for what they do?
  7. Equius treats Nepeta’s swearing as something she picked up from trolls he considers beneath her. What parallels do you see in how people judge someone’s speech or friendships as signs of social class?
  8. Does beating the Black King or creating a universe count as winning Sgrub if the trolls never get to claim the reward? What would you count as a win?

Trivia & Details

  • Hivebent’s opening equates six solar sweeps with thirteen Earth years. The trolls are introduced at the same age as the four human players.
  • Each of the twelve introduced trolls has a six-letter first name and a six-letter surname. Their trolltag initials use only A, C, G, and T, the letters representing DNA’s four bases.
  • The four romance quadrants use the four playing-card suits: hearts for matespritship, diamonds for moirallegiance, spades for kismesissitude, and clubs for auspisticism.
  • Karkat’s rejection of poetry and mailbox arms reworks imagery from John’s earlier outdoor monologue.
