Reading overview

Jade and Prospit

  • Jade Harley lives on a remote island with her dog Becquerel. She uses colored strings to remember her errands and the packages she expects to arrive.
  • When Jade falls asleep, her dreamself flies over Prospit while her dreambot moves around on Earth. She sees visions in Skaia’s clouds. John’s dreamself lives in the neighboring tower and has yet to wake up.
  • In the future, the Peregrine Mendicant and Wayward Vagabond help send a blue package into the past. John’s gift reaches Jade when she is still a little girl, years before he sends it.

Rose’s Entry and John’s First Gate

  • John searches Dad’s room and realizes he was wrong about his father being a professional clown. He makes new gear with alchemy while Rose builds his house up toward the first gate.
  • Rose finds a Skaianet lab that tracks Sburb sessions and incoming meteors. Trying to retrieve a living Jaspers produces a mutated clone. She later retrieves Jaspers’s original body and uses it to prototype her sprite.
  • Bro leaves the Sburb discs behind after his rooftop fight with Dave. Dave connects as Rose’s server player, and she enters the Medium before the meteor strikes.
  • John jumps through his first gate. Jade enters the frog ruins on her island and finds Sburb discs in a lotus time capsule.

The Midnight Crew’s Raid

  • After the Felt hit one of their casinos, Spades Slick and the Midnight Crew raid the Felt’s mansion to find Lord English’s vault.
  • The Felt use time powers against the Crew. Trace can follow where someone has been and Fin can follow where they are going. The Crew avoids killing Snowman because her death would destroy the universe.

Inside the Vault

  • Slick pries open the vault and is thrown into another timeline, where the mansion is in ruins and almost everyone is dead.
  • Snowman tears off Slick’s barcoded arm and locks him inside. He flips his character graphic to move the barcode to his remaining arm, then scans it to open a hatch.
  • Below the hatch, Slick finds a command station with a troll on its screen and starts typing commands.

Reading notes

  • Jade’s introduction is so good. I love that a girl who can see some things coming still needs strings tied around her fingers to remember what to do about them. The strings make her foreknowledge feel like one more thing she has to keep track of.
  • Who’s giving the commands? The story asks us to “be” a character as if we’ve just picked them in a game. Then a command tells Jade to give up an interest, and she answers with a whole fantasy about becoming a wolf. I love how quickly the command gets drowned out by what Jade actually cares about.
  • The dreambot is a bridge. One page pairs Jade in her dream room with her robot standing beside her sleeping body. Seeing both rooms together makes Prospit feel as much a part of her life as her room on Earth.
  • Look at the clocks. I love that the Intermission turns time into something you can track around a room. Slick realizes that a clock he saw broken in the future is still ticking in front of him. Now the reader and Slick are waiting for the same moment.
  • The body jokes are rough. When Sawbuck is wounded, he and the people nearby jump to another time. That’s a great problem for Slick to solve. The jokes about Sawbuck’s size pull me out of a scene that already has a funny setup.

Discussion questions

  1. What surprised you most about Jade once we saw what her life on the island was like?
  2. What does seeing Jade on Prospit explain about the things she seems to know ahead of time on Earth?
  3. What does Slick figure out when he sees the same clock broken and then intact? How does he use that information?
  4. Slick looks like Jack Noir and finds a command station like the exiles’. What connection do you think the story is setting up?
  5. Jade ties strings around her fingers to remember what to do and when. What do you use to remind yourself at the right moment? What do you still have to remember for yourself?
  6. When Jade refuses the command to give up her interest in animals, does it feel like she’s arguing with her own thoughts or with someone giving her instructions? What makes you read it that way?
  7. The fight with Sawbuck mixes time jumps with jokes about his size. Which parts of the scene are funny to you? Where does the humor lose you?
  8. After seeing the Intermission spell out its time jumps with clocks and trails, what clues do you notice in Act 3 about the order in which things happen?

Trivia & Details

  • Becquerel is a unit as well as a dog. Jade’s pet is named Becquerel. A becquerel measures radioactive activity: one decay per second. It is a tidy connection with the comic’s recurring nuclear imagery.
  • The inventory joke survives the cast change. Asked to captchalogue a clock, Slick does not recognize the term. The interface is familiar to us, but the character does not share the kids’ vocabulary.
  • The tailor repairs the fabric of spacetime. Time travel causes tears in Lord English’s coat, which Stitch has to mend. The familiar phrase becomes an actual tailoring problem.
  • Easy inventory is boring. John’s array modus gives him direct access to any card. The narration immediately calls it boring and notes that it’s hard to use as a weapon.
