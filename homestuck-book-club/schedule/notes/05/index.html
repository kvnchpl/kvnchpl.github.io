Reading overview

Jade’s Entry

  • Karkat begins trolling a John who already knows him. Rose and Kanaya work through their out-of-order conversations, while Dave uses time loops to help the other kids.
  • John retrieves his server disc and connects as Jade’s server player.
  • Bec’s prototyping saves Jade from the meteor, but also gives Jack First Guardian powers, making him a threat to both sessions.

Ascension

  • Rose plans to destroy the Green Sun, hoping to cut off Jack’s powers. Dave would help her navigate there; her dreamself would not survive the mission.
  • Vriska guides John to his Quest Bed. Jack kills him there, and his dreamself ascends to god tier.
  • Terezi shows Dave a possible route to god tier involving a doomed version of himself. Dave refuses to kill that other self.
  • A flashback reveals Vriska’s earlier ascension: Tavros leaves her dying on her quest cocoon, and she returns at god tier.

Creating Jadesprite

  • With Dave’s replacement equipment and shared grist, Jade makes new weapons and tools on the Land of Frost and Frogs.
  • Looking for a way to fight Jack, Jade combines her dead dreamself with Becsprite, creating Jadesprite.
  • Jadesprite is frightened and overwhelmed by being brought back.

Reading notes

  • The John/Karkat reversal is so satisfying. Karkat expects an easy target and instead meets a John who already treats him as a friend. We are finally seeing Karkat begin a relationship that John has already experienced. His embarrassment makes the earlier insults feel different: the hostility is tangled up with affection.
  • Kanaya and Rose finally catch up. Phew. Their eighth conversation is the eighth for both of them. Both can now remember the seven conversations that led up to it. After so much withholding and sarcastic maneuvering, I like the relief of seeing them finally reach that point.
  • The old logistical problems still matter. The server disc stranded in Dad’s car becomes useful at last. The problem of getting hold of a game disc still matters in a story now concerned with making universes. Following a familiar object helps me connect the current events to the much smaller problems that started the story.
  • Destiny is becoming a moral argument. In her explanation of the Jack disaster, Vriska argues that she could not simply prevent an event that had already happened. That leaves another question: does knowing the outcome excuse the choices she made to bring it about?
  • More power does not mean the same thing to everyone. John’s ascension looks triumphant, while Dave refuses to kill his doomed self as part of Terezi’s proposed route to god tier. Jade has a different plan for gaining power: bringing back her dreamself as a sprite. But Jadesprite’s distress makes the cost to that dreamself impossible to ignore. Jade wants a powerful helper; the person she brings back is frightened and does not want to be there.

Open threads

  • Reaching the Green Sun. Rose says she’ll navigate the Furthest Ring alone to carry out the plan. Whether she can reach the Sun and make it back is still open.
  • Dream bubbles. Feferi tells Jade she’ll ask the horrorterrors to make dream bubbles so people can meet in their sleep. Jade and Feferi have now met in one, but it’s still unclear who else can visit.
  • Jadesprite’s place in the group. Jade brings back her dreamself to help fight Jack, but Jadesprite is distraught about being brought back. It’s still unclear what she wants for herself or whether she’ll help.

Discussion questions

  1. Which old object or running joke had the most satisfying payoff in this section?
  2. How did you react to Karkat trying to troll a John who already thinks of him as a friend?
  3. Rose and Kanaya can both remember their previous seven conversations when they reach the eighth. What does finally sharing that history let them say or do differently?
  4. How is Rose’s plan supposed to stop Jack, and what roles do John and Dave have in it?
  5. What do you think will happen when the trolls’ countdown reaches zero? Which clues support your guess?
  6. Does the fact that an event has already happened excuse helping to cause it? How convincing is Vriska’s defense of her role in Jack’s rise?
  7. Compare John’s ascension with Dave’s refusal to kill his doomed self. What counts as success in each scene?
  8. Where do you see echoes of reincarnation in John’s death on the Quest Bed and his return at god tier? How does making rebirth a way to gain power change what it means in the story?
  9. Jade wants to help her friends by creating Jadesprite, but Jadesprite does not want to be brought back. What makes a rescue successful when the rescuer and the person rescued want different things?

Trivia & Details

  • The bucket joke has an earlier explanation. Vriska’s mock embarrassment at John’s cleaning bucket plays on the reproductive role of pails explained during Hivebent. Her claim that cleaning products themselves are indecent is part of her teasing.
  • A literal hood for godhood. I read the hood on John’s new outfit as a visual pun on “godhood.” It is a small joke tucked inside a major transformation.
  • Feferi announces her death without explaining it. She answers Jade’s question about why she is there by declaring that she is dead. She does not explain when or how she died.
