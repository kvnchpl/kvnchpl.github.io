Act 5 Act 2 (Part 1): He is already here. Start Page: 2626 # of Pages: 613

Reading overview

The humans’ and trolls’ stories reconnect. Karkat’s first conversation with John is a later conversation for John, while Rose and Kanaya work through their own scrambled introductions. Dave exploits time loops to help the session, and John finally connects as Jade’s server player.

Bec’s prototyping saves Jade but gives Jack first guardian powers. Rose plans a mission to destroy the Green Sun; John reaches god tier; Dave refuses Terezi’s proposed route there. Jade’s attempt to create a powerful helper instead produces a distressed Jadesprite. The assigned reading ends with the author’s recap at page 3238, before the trolls’ countdown is resolved.

> Read Recap #5.

Reading notes

  • The John/Karkat reversal is so satisfying. Karkat expects an easy target and instead meets a John who already treats him as a friend. We are finally seeing Karkat begin a relationship that John has already experienced. His embarrassment makes the earlier insults feel different: the hostility is tangled up with affection.
  • Kanaya and Rose finally catch up. Phew. Their eighth conversation is the eighth for both of them. Both can now remember the seven conversations that led up to it. After so much withholding and sarcastic maneuvering, I like the relief of seeing them finally reach that point.
  • The old logistical problems still matter. The server disc stranded in Dad’s car becomes useful at last. The problem of getting hold of a game disc still matters in a story now concerned with making universes. Following a familiar object helps me connect the current events to the much smaller problems that started the story.
  • Destiny is becoming a moral argument. In her explanation of the Jack disaster, Vriska argues that she could not simply prevent an event that had already happened. That leaves another question: does knowing the outcome excuse the choices she made to bring it about?
  • More power does not mean the same thing to everyone. John’s ascension looks triumphant, while Dave refuses to kill his doomed self as part of Terezi’s proposed route to god tier. Jade has a different plan for gaining power: bringing back her dreamself as a sprite. But Jadesprite’s distress makes the cost to that dreamself impossible to ignore. Jade wants a powerful helper; the person she brings back is frightened and does not want to be there.

Discussion questions

  1. Which old object or running joke had the most satisfying payoff in this section?
  2. How did you react to Karkat trying to troll a John who already thinks of him as a friend?
  3. Rose and Kanaya can both remember their previous seven conversations when they reach the eighth. What does finally sharing that history let them say or do differently?
  4. How much control does Rose have over her plan to destroy the Green Sun, given whose advice she relies on?
  5. Does the fact that an event has already happened excuse helping to cause it? How convincing is Vriska’s defense of her role in Jack’s rise?
  6. Compare John’s ascension with Dave’s refusal to kill his doomed self. What counts as success in each scene?
  7. Jade wants to help her friends by creating Jadesprite, but Jadesprite does not want to be brought back. What makes a rescue successful when the rescuer and the person rescued want different things?

Trivia & Details

  • The bucket joke has an earlier explanation. Vriska’s mock embarrassment at John’s cleaning bucket plays on the reproductive role of pails explained during Hivebent. Her claim that cleaning products themselves are indecent is part of her teasing.
  • Becquerel is a unit as well as a dog. Jade’s pet is named Becquerel. A becquerel measures radioactive activity: one decay per second. It is a tidy connection with the comic’s recurring nuclear imagery.
  • A literal hood for godhood. I read the hood on John’s new outfit as a visual pun on “godhood.” It is a small joke tucked inside a major transformation.
  • Feferi announces her death without explaining it. She answers Jade’s question about why she is there by declaring that she is dead. She does not explain when or how she died. At this point, I want to distinguish what she tells Jade from what we have actually seen happen.
