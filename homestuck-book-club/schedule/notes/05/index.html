Act 5 Act 2 (Part 1)

Quick overview

The humans’ and trolls’ stories reconnect. Karkat’s first conversation with John is a later conversation for John, while Rose and Kanaya work through their own scrambled introductions. Dave exploits time loops to help the session, and John finally connects as Jade’s server player.

Bec’s prototyping saves Jade but gives Jack first guardian powers. Rose plans a mission to destroy the Green Sun; John reaches god tier; Dave refuses Terezi’s proposed route there. Jade’s attempt to create a powerful helper instead produces a distressed Jadesprite. The assigned reading ends with the author’s recap at page 3238, before the trolls’ countdown is resolved.

Reading notes

  • The John/Karkat reversal is so satisfying. Karkat expects an easy target and instead meets a John who already treats him as a friend (p. 2628). The original trolling now looks like a relationship whose participants cannot agree about where it begins. Embarrassment and affection complicate the supposed hostility.
  • Kanaya and Rose finally catch up. Phew. Their eighth conversation is the eighth for both of them (p. 2721). I like the relief of reaching the same conversational present after all that tactical omission and competitive sarcasm.
  • The old logistical problems still matter. The server disc stranded in Dad’s car becomes useful at last (p. 2655). A household mishap from Act 1 still has consequences in a story now concerned with making universes. The callbacks help, even when the timeline remains ridiculous.
  • Destiny is becoming a moral argument. Vriska’s claim that an established event cannot simply be undone does not settle whether helping cause it was defensible. Her explanation of the Jack disaster is a good place to separate causal consistency from responsibility (p. 2975).
  • Progress does not feel equally heroic for everyone. John’s ascension, Dave’s refusal to kill another Dave, and Jade’s failed rescue plan give very different meanings to advancement. Jadesprite’s distress makes it especially hard to treat a person as just a new power-up (p. 3096, p. 3238).

Discussion questions

Pick a few that catch the group’s interest. No need to work through the whole list.

  1. Revisit the card-suit imagery in the opening animation. What might it suggest about Karkat’s expectations of John, and how does that compare with John’s side of the conversation?
  2. Which human/troll relationship changed most for you when we returned to the humans’ session?
  3. How does seeing Karkat’s first conversation with John change the way their earlier conversations read? What part do embarrassment and affection play?
  4. What changes when Rose and Kanaya finally share the same conversational present? Does their sarcasm still serve the same purpose?
  5. Do the backlinks and repeated conversations help you follow the chronology, or make you more conscious of how much you cannot keep straight?
  6. Which old object or running joke has the most satisfying payoff in this section? Why does the server disc still feel worth caring about?
  7. How does Dave’s use of time travel change what counts as work, obligation, or personal experience? What does another Dave’s death mean to him?
  8. How do Vriska and Terezi differ as guides to the kids? Where does helping become control, competition, or manipulation?
  9. If an event is already part of the timeline, does that change a character’s responsibility for bringing it about? How persuasive do you find Vriska’s defense?
  10. Rose wants to reject the game’s prescribed path. How independent does her alternative plan feel when it also relies on advice from powerful, interested parties?
  11. Compare John accepting Vriska’s help to ascend with Dave refusing to kill his doomed self. What kinds of courage or hesitation does each scene make visible?
  12. Jade wants to save everyone, while Jadesprite is overwhelmed by being brought back. How does their conflict complicate the idea of a successful rescue?
  13. What do we actually know at page 3238, and what remains a plan, prediction, or unexplained event? Which uncertainty feels most urgent?

Trivia & details to bring up

  • The bucket joke has an earlier explanation. Vriska’s mock embarrassment at John’s cleaning bucket (p. 2674) plays on the reproductive role of pails explained during Hivebent (p. 2396). Her claim that cleaning products themselves are indecent is part of her teasing.
  • Becquerel is a unit as well as a dog. Jade’s pet is named in p. 791. A becquerel measures radioactive activity: one decay per second (EPA definition). It is a tidy connection with the comic’s recurring nuclear imagery.
  • God tier comes with a god hood. John’s new outfit makes the wordplay visible (p. 3087). It is a small joke tucked inside a major transformation.
  • Trollian does not make its user omniscient. At the cutoff, Feferi has told Jade she is dead, but the circumstances of her own death have not been shown. The distinction matters when discussing predictions and partial viewpoints. Keep the open questions open (assigned ending recap).
