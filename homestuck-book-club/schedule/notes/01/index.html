Reading overview

John’s Birthday

  • On his thirteenth birthday, John Egbert waits for the Sburb beta and struggles to get things back out of his card-based inventory.
  • Dad has already brought in the mail. John gets past him and opens his packages, finding the game and the stuffed bunny from Con Air. The server disc and Jade’s birthday package for John are still locked in Dad’s car.

The Meteor Countdown

  • Rose connects as John’s server player. She can move objects around his house and deploy machines there.
  • Opening the Cruxtruder starts a countdown and releases a kernelsprite. Rose drops a damaged harlequin doll into the sprite, giving it the doll’s features.
  • With a meteor about to hit and Rose’s connection failing in a storm, they use the machines to make a blue apple. John bites the apple, and the house vanishes just before impact.

John in the Medium

  • John’s house reappears on a pillar above the clouds. The sprite absorbs his grandmother’s ashes and becomes Nannasprite. She explains Skaia and its war and tells John that Dad has been taken. Rose needs to build the house up to the first gate.
  • Rose tries to lift Dad’s car back to the house, but her laptop dies and the car falls with the server disc inside. John asks Dave to help Rose get into the game instead.
  • A crow takes Dave’s copies of Sburb. When Dave tries to recover them, his sword sends the crow and the discs out the window. He looks for more copies in Bro’s apartment.
  • Rose runs a generator at Jaspers’s mausoleum to reconnect to John. He uses grist to make new equipment and faces two ogres as the fire cuts Rose’s connection again.

The Wayward Vagabond and Can Town

  • Years in the future, the Wayward Vagabond finds a station with John on a monitor. He can type commands that John hears.
  • WV builds Can Town out of his food supplies. He also finds an appearifier and uses it to free a firefly from amber.
  • The station’s countdown ends, and it launches out of the ground, carrying WV to a set of frog ruins. Elsewhere, Mom opens a passage under Jaspers’s mausoleum, Dad escapes his captors, and Dave meets Bro on the roof.

Reading notes

  • Who is “you”? I love how “you” can sound like a character’s own thoughts or like someone telling them what to do. Rose won’t fuss over her private journals while someone is watching. When a command to get cookies trails off, the comic cuts to a finger slipping on an actual keyboard. The command has a person behind it, and John can still refuse it.
  • They know things we do not. The kids already know how to use a sylladex, even when it gives them trouble. I’m still catching up. Once Rose has to read a walkthrough to help John, we get to learn Sburb’s rules with them.
  • Rooms as character introductions. Before John leaves his room, we know he’s an amateur magician who loves terrible movies. Then we see Dad’s harlequins through John’s eyes. I’m curious how much they tell us about Dad and how much they tell us about what John notices.
  • The irony has levels, apparently. Dave admires the effort Bro puts into keeping a Mr. T puppet ironically. Later he wonders whether some of the stuff Bro leaves out is a test of whether he gets the joke. The more Dave wants to be in on it, the harder it is to tell what he actually enjoys.
  • Even a new name changes the rules. Dave calls his skateboard a “wheeled ride” to get it into his sylladex. I like the parallel with alchemy. John combines codes to make new objects, and Dave changes how the sylladex handles an object by giving it a new name.

Discussion questions

  1. What detail in John’s, Rose’s, or Dave’s room gave you the strongest first impression of them?
  2. What does John have to do before the meteor hits? Which parts can he only do with Rose’s help?
  3. What clues connect WV’s wasteland to the world the kids live in? What do you think happened between their story and his?
  4. What do the kids assume about their guardians? Did a scene make you trust or question one of those assumptions?
  5. Dave tries to keep up with Bro’s irony. When does sharing an inside joke help people feel closer, and when does getting the joke start to feel like a test?
  6. WV gives his cans a town plan and declares them all equal. What makes Can Town feel like a community to him? What makes a real place feel like a community to you?
  7. After seeing WV at the terminal, which commands would you read differently? Who do you think is giving the commands that don’t seem to come from him?
  8. How do countdowns, inventory rules, and jokes change the way you feel about the meteor threat?

Trivia & Details

  • The fake literary credits are a running joke. The fireplace passage credits Mark Twain, but the moon stealing its light comes from Shakespeare’s Timon of Athens, Act 4, scene 3. Rose’s rainy introduction credits Charles Barkley for the opening of T. S. Eliot’s The Waste Land. The narrator’s certainty is part of the joke.
  • [S] starts with a piano. John’s piano refrain introduces [S] as the marker for pages with sound. The same marker appears on the animation at the end of Act 2, which cuts between five cliffhangers.
  • A word can change the mechanics. Dave gets his battery pack accepted by treating Y as a consonant instead of a vowel. The punchline depends on wording, rather than a new item or a stronger weapon.
