Act 4: Flight of the Paradox Clones Start Page: 1358 # of Pages: 631

Reading overview

John explores his Land while the kids learn how their quests, gates, dreamselves, and server connections fit together. A doomed future Dave returns to help prevent John’s death, becoming Davesprite. Jack Noir seizes power, and the exiles’ earlier lives begin to connect to the ruins we already know.

John’s ectobiology work reveals that the kids and their guardians are tangled in their own origins. The act closes with a battle on the Battlefield, the death of Jade’s dreamself, and John waking there. Two in-story recaps help hold the increasingly knotted structure together.

> Read Recap #3.

Reading notes

  • The trolls do not know everything. Knowing more than the kids does not stop them from being awkward, confused, or wrong. CG and John’s developing friendship is especially funny because they are encountering it in different orders. Each expects the other to remember things that have not happened to him yet. I am becoming less willing to trust someone just because they can see the future.
  • GA and Rose turn conversation into a puzzle. Their mismatched conversation counts mean they cannot even agree on when they first met. GA tries to engineer a friendship through the sarcastic exchanges she already knows will happen. I read some of that hostility as an attempt at connection. Does it feel like courtship, friendship, or both?
  • Computer-savvy problem solving. Dave uses his alchemiter to copy Rose’s journals, while a program called GristTorrent borrows the language of file sharing. I like how the game applies familiar computer habits to physical objects and resources. The kids look for ways to exploit its rules, just as they might look for a shortcut in software.
  • Ectobiological weirdness. John helps create the babies who will become the kids and their guardians, including himself. Their origins form a loop: the person doing the creating is also one of the people being created. The recurring family roles and relationships remind me of Cloud Atlas.
  • The story can interrupt itself to explain itself. The first recap, author interlude, and second recap make jokes out of the work of explaining the plot. I find that funny partly because I really do need the help. As more connections are revealed, the summaries let us reconsider earlier scenes with information we did not have the first time.

Discussion questions

  1. Which Act 4 reveal made you most want to reread an earlier scene?
  2. Which Land feels most revealing about its player, and what detail gives you that impression?
  3. What does Rose gain, and what does she risk, by resisting the game’s intended path?
  4. When a troll or a future self knows more than the kids, what makes their advice worth trusting?
  5. Do you think of Dave and Davesprite as the same person? What does the doomed timeline change about your answer?
  6. John helps create himself and his family. How does that change the meaning of “parent” and “child” in this story?
  7. What changes when the author becomes a character in the story he is telling?

Trivia & Details

  • Jade’s drawing interface fits the comic’s medium. Jade draws her lunchtop on the Pictionary scribblepad, and the next page shows it being captchalogued. Drawing is how she stores the object. The switch to this modus is described as a democratic choice. In a comic already so invested in drawing, making it an inventory mechanic feels especially fitting.
  • AND and OR are different experiments. Dave combines the same ingredients with && and || and gets different results. It is a nice concrete example of a small change in notation creating a different object.
  • Grist has its own file-sharing joke. The software is called GristTorrent. Even the material used to make objects gets a name that makes it sound like something you could download.
