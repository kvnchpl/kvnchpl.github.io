Act 4
September 7, 2026

Reading observations and prompts to bring to our conversation.

Overview Reading notes Questions Trivia

Quick overview

John explores his Land while the kids learn how their quests, gates, dreamselves, and server connections fit together. A doomed future Dave returns to help prevent John’s death, becoming Davesprite. Jack Noir seizes power, and the exiles’ earlier lives begin to connect to the ruins we already know.

John’s ectobiology work reveals that the kids and their guardians are tangled in their own origins. The act closes with a battle on the Battlefield, the death of Jade’s dreamself, and John waking there. Two in-story recaps help hold the increasingly knotted structure together.

Reading notes

  • The trolls are losing their air of invulnerability. Their disagreements, awkwardness, and contradictory knowledge are starting to show. CG and John’s developing friendship is especially funny because its two participants are encountering it in different orders (p. 1667). I am reading the apparent authority of the future more skeptically now.
  • GA and Rose’s conversational artifice. Their mismatched conversation counts turn getting acquainted into a puzzle (p. 1592). I read some of the hostility as a possible attempt at connection. Does that come across as courtship, or as two people trying to understand each other?
  • Computer-savvy problem solving. Dave’s interest in copying Rose’s journals and the grist-sharing software feels connected to a piracy-informed habit of working around interfaces (p. 1695, p. 1520). The game’s rules become material to exploit, rather than instructions to obey.
  • Ectobiological weirdness. The origin story collapses ancestry into a loop instead of a neat family tree. The repeated dramas among versions of these characters remind me of Cloud Atlas, as a comparison rather than a claimed influence.
  • The story can interrupt itself to explain itself. The recaps and author interlude are funny partly because we really do need help (p. 1674, p. 1678, p. 1988). The later revelation does not just add information; it makes an earlier scene mean something different.

Discussion questions

Pick a few that catch the group’s interest. No need to work through the whole list.

  1. Which reveal in Act 4 most changed your understanding of an earlier scene?
  2. Which character's storyline became more interesting to you during this act?
  3. Which of the characters' Lands feels most closely connected to its player?
  4. Which recurring object or image now feels most significant?
  5. How do John, Rose, Dave, and Jade differ in their responses to the expectations of Sburb?
  6. How does Rose's resistance to the game's intended path affect your understanding of her?
  7. What does Davesprite reveal about Dave that his usual behavior tends to conceal?
  8. How has Jack Noir's development changed the role he seems to occupy in the story?
  9. How does learning the histories of the Exiles change the way their earlier scenes read?
  10. What does the ectobiology sequence do to conventional ideas of family and origin?
  11. How do doomed timelines complicate the idea of individual choice?
  12. What role do circulating objects play in holding the increasingly complex story together?
  13. How does Homestuck make causal loops understandable despite telling events out of chronological order?
  14. What changes when the boundary between the fictional world and its author becomes part of the fiction itself?
  15. How do the interactive exploration and map pages change your sense of space compared with the fixed panels?
  16. When a future character knows more, do we treat them as more trustworthy? Where has that assumption already failed?

Trivia & details to bring up

  • Jade’s drawing interface fits the comic’s medium. The Pictionary modus asks her to draw an item to retrieve it (p. 1374). The preceding page invokes democracy in describing the choice (p. 1372). In a comic already so invested in drawing, retrieving things by drawing them feels especially fitting.
  • AND and OR are different experiments. Dave combines the same ingredients with && and || and gets different results (p. 1837, p. 1838). It is a nice concrete example of a small change in notation creating a different object.
  • Grist has its own file-sharing joke. The software is called GristTorrent (p. 1520). Alongside the journal-copying scheme, it gives the game’s resource economy a distinctly desktop-computer sensibility.
